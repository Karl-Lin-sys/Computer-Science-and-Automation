# Pedagogy and Bias Mitigation Framework

## 1. Mitigation of the Fundamental Attribution Error
The fundamental attribution error is the tendency to attribute others' actions to their character or personality, while attributing one's own behavior to external situational factors. In Computer Science, this often manifests as blaming "user error" for system failures or attributing algorithmic failures to "bad data" without examining structural design flaws.

### Implementation in Curriculum:
- **Systems Thinking Over Individual Blame**: Students are trained through RCT-validated case studies to evaluate failures at the systemic level (e.g., interface design, structural incentives) rather than attributing fault to end-users.
- **Algorithmic Accountability**: In AI and ML, errors are framed within sociotechnical contexts (based on recent Systematic Reviews) rather than purely mathematical oversights.

## 2. Strict Evidence Thresholds
The doctoral program relies exclusively on two tiers of empirical evidence:
- **Synthetic Evidence (≤ 3 years)**: Meta-analyses and Systematic Reviews ensure that the broader paradigms taught are resistant to single-study anomalies and publication bias.
- **Primary Evidence (≤ 20 years)**: Randomized Controlled Trials (RCTs) are used to validate specific interventions, teaching methods, and algorithm efficacy where blinded, controlled environments are necessary.
